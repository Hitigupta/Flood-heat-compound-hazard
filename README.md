# Urban Heat and Flooding as Compound Hazards
### A multi-source, explainable machine-learning framework for compound risk assessment and intervention planning

## What this project does

Cities face extreme heat and flooding in the same year, and the same urban features (concrete, lost vegetation, poor drainage) drive both. Most studies model them separately. This project:

1. Puts heat, flood, terrain, land-cover and weather data for **Patna, Bihar** on one common 30 m grid
2. Trains interpretable **XGBoost** models for heat risk and flood risk
3. Explains both with **SHAP** to find the shared structural causes
4. Combines the two into a **compound risk score** that includes an interaction term
5. Uses the explanations to suggest **targeted interventions** (more green cover, permeable surfaces, drainage upgrades)

Aligned with **SDG 11** (Sustainable Cities and Communities) and **SDG 13** (Climate Action).

## How to run (Google Colab)

1. Open [colab.research.google.com](https://colab.research.google.com) and upload `notebooks/01_data_acquisition.ipynb` (File > Upload notebook).
2. Sign up for [Google Earth Engine](https://earthengine.google.com) (free for research) and create a Google Cloud project.
3. In cell 2, replace `YOUR-GCP-PROJECT-ID` with your project ID.
4. In cell 3, check the event dates against news reports or Copernicus EMS.
5. Run all cells from top to bottom. The GeoTIFF is exported to Google Drive under `patna_compound/`.

## Method summary

- **Labels:** heat = hot pixels from Landsat surface temperature; flood = Sentinel-1 backscatter drop (change detection), with permanent water removed
- **Features:** static (slope, elevation, HAND, built-up, vegetation, building height) and dynamic (3-day and 7-day rainfall, heatwave days)
- **Models:** XGBoost for each hazard, spatial block cross-validation, probability calibration
- **Metrics:** ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, Brier score, reliability curves
- **Explainability:** SHAP for both models, then compare drivers
- **Compound risk:** `C = H + F + lambda * (H x F)` with H and F scaled to 0-1, plus sensitivity analysis on lambda

## Known limitations

- The heatwave count is a proxy (days with maximum temperature of at least 40 C), not the official IMD definition
- The Patna flood label mixes river flooding with urban waterlogging
- SAR flood detection is less reliable in dense built-up areas
- Rainfall and weather data are 5 to 11 km, so they give one value per event rather than a map
- The Landsat thermal band is natively 100 m, resampled to 30 m
- There is no ground truth for compound risk, so it is validated through sensitivity analysis
- Event dates were chosen from memory of past events and must be verified before results are reported
